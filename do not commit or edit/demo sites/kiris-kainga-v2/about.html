Ko wai au — Who I am

Kiri's
Story

I grew up on this peninsula with bare feet and salty hair, listening to my kuia tell me about the old days — when our hapū moved with the seasons, when every rock on the headland had a name, when the sea was our road and our larder and our mirror.

I left when I was eighteen to study at the University of Auckland. I graduated with a degree in Māori Studies and Education, then taught for seven years in Tāmaki Makaurau. And then a Japanese exchange student asked me a simple question: "What does it actually feel like to be Māori?"

I couldn't answer that in a classroom. So I came home. In 2016, with help from my whānau, I converted my grandmother's property into Kiri's Kāinga — a place where people from anywhere in the world could live the answer to that question, not as theory, but as breath, as fire, as earth, as song.

We have now welcomed guests from over 40 countries. I have watched people arrive as strangers and leave as something closer to whānau. That transformation — that is why I do this.

What We
Stand For

🤝

Manaakitanga

Hospitality and care — showing respect, love, and generosity for all who come through our door, regardless of where they're from.

🌱

Kaitiakitanga

Guardianship of the natural environment. We operate carbon-consciously, use zero-waste practices, and plant native trees for every booking.

🏔

Whakapapa

Everything is connected through genealogy — people, land, sky, and sea. We help guests understand their own place in the web of the world.

🎶

Whanaungatanga

Community and belonging. At Kiri's Kāinga, you are never a tourist — you are a temporary member of something real.

💬

Kōrero Pono

Honest conversation. We share the full complexity of Māori history — not just the beautiful parts — with openness and integrity.

🌊

Ūkaipō

The place of nourishing — the source of belonging. Our land is our ūkaipō, and we are honoured to share it with the world.

How Kiri's
Kāinga Grew

1987

Born on the Peninsula

Raised in her kuia's house — surrounded by kōrero, birds, and kai from the sea.

2009

University & Teaching

BA in Māori Studies at University of Auckland, then seven years teaching in Tāmaki Makaurau.

2015

The Question

"What does it actually feel like to be Māori?" A student's question changes everything. Kiri comes home.

2016

Kiri's Kāinga Opens

Two rooms, one dream. The first hāngi is held on a Friday in November under a full moon.
