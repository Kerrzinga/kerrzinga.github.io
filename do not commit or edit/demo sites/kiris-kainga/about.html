Meet Kiri

The woman behind the kāinga — her whakapapa, her vision, and her lifelong love of this land.

Kiri Tūhoe Host & Cultural Guide · Ngāti Manuhiri descent · Born on the Whangaparaoa Peninsula
Ko wai au — Who I am

Kiri's Story

I grew up on this peninsula with bare feet and salty hair, listening to my kuia (grandmother) tell me about the old days — when our hapū moved with the seasons, when every rock on the headland had a name, when the sea was our road and our larder and our mirror.

I left when I was eighteen to study at the University of Auckland, graduating with a degree in Māori Studies and Education. I taught for seven years in Tāmaki Makaurau (Auckland) before a simple question from a foreign student — "What does it actually feel like to be Māori?" — changed everything.

I couldn't answer that question in a classroom. So I came home. In 2016, with help from my whānau, I converted my grandmother's property into Kiri's Kāinga: a place where people from anywhere in the world could experience the answer to that question not as theory, but as lived reality — as breath, as fire, as earth, as song.

In the years since, we've welcomed guests from over 40 countries. I've watched people arrive as strangers and leave as something closer to whānau. That transformation — that is why I do this.

Ōu uaratanga — Our values

What We Stand For

Everything at Kiri's Kāinga flows from four core values inherited from tikanga Māori.

🤝

Manaakitanga

The principle of hospitality and generosity — showing respect, care, and love for all who come through our door, regardless of where they come from.

🌱

Kaitiakitanga

Guardianship of the natural environment. We operate carbon-consciously, use zero-waste practices, and plant native trees for every booking made.

🏔

Whakapapa

Everything is connected through genealogy — people, land, sky, and sea. We help guests understand their own place in the web of the world.

🎶

Whanaungatanga

The deep sense of community and belonging. At Kiri's Kāinga, you are never a tourist — you are a temporary member of something real.

💬

Kōrero Pono

Honest, truthful conversation. We share the full complexity of Māori history — not just the beautiful parts — with openness and integrity.

🌊

Ūkaipō

The place of nurturing — the source of nourishment and belonging. Our land is our ūkaipō, and we are honoured to share it with the world.

Our journey

How Kiri's Kāinga Grew

1987

Kiri is born on the Peninsula

Raised in her grandmother's house on what is now the property — surrounded by kuia, kōrero, and kai from the sea.

2009

University graduation

Kiri completes her BA in Māori Studies at the University of Auckland, then begins teaching in Māngere.

2015

The question that changed everything

A Japanese exchange student asks: "What does it actually feel like to be Māori?" Kiri can't answer in a classroom. She decides to build the answer instead.

2016

Kiri's Kāinga opens

With two rooms and a dream, Kiri welcomes her first international guests. The first hāngi is held on a Friday in November under a full moon.

2019

Tourism NZ Award

Kiri's Kāinga wins the Tourism New Zealand Māori Cultural Experience Award and is featured in Lonely Planet's Best in Travel.

2022

Expansion & community hubs

The Kauri Wharepuni is completed. Kiri partners with three neighbouring whānau to offer expanded experiences across the Peninsula.

Today

40+ countries welcomed

Over 1,200 guests have experienced Kiri's Kāinga since opening. The fire pit is always lit.
